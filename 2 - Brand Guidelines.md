# 2. Brand Guidelines

Wright Co. 2026-10-15. Version 0.1, draft. Prepared by BlakSheep Creative.

**Status.** A BlakSheep Creative proposal built from the owner's intake answers, public records, a web-presence review and a scan of the local competitors. The owner, Cody Wright, has not reviewed it and has approved none of it. The emblem and the colors sampled from it are the client's own. The tagline, the three offer names, the typefaces, the corner radius, the palette roles and the button pair are decisions BlakSheep Creative has made, and they are usable now while the owner's sign-off is pending. Positioning, purpose, mission, vision, values and personality are derived from research: they guide the work, and none of them is published as a statement until the owner accepts it. Every promise or fact only the owner can make is marked `CONFIRM BEFORE PUBLISHING`: it may be set into a layout with the marker visible, and it is not published until he answers.

## The binding limits

These apply to every sentence and every design made for the company. If a line or a layout breaks one, it goes.

1. The name is "Wright Co.", with the period. Never "LLC", "Wright Co. LLC", "Wright Co LLC" or "WrightCo". The `.llc` in wrightco.llc is a web address, not part of the name.
2. No "licensed", "insured", "bonded", license number or credential of any kind.
3. One history claim only: "clearing and excavating since 2022", with no place named. No years in Hammond or Louisiana, no "established", no "Est. 2022", no "local since", no "decades of experience", no "founded".
4. No star rating, no review count, no "5-star", no "top-rated". None exists to quote.
5. Nothing from the owner's Washington business, Wright Excavation LLC: no testimonials, no photos, no reviews, no links, no mention.
6. No street address. The place line is "Serving Hammond, Tangipahoa Parish & the Northshore".
7. No price, range, per-acre figure, discount or "affordable". Quotes come through the Free Lot Walk or an estimate.
8. No "#1", "premier", "best", "trusted", "top-rated", "leading" or "quality" as a claim.
9. Nothing a competitor says: no "standing pine", no "slab", no "every phase", no "one contractor", no "raw land to finished build", and never "start to finish" on its own.
10. No pad heights, elevations or flood promises. No "we handle your permits". The surveyor and the parish set the numbers; Wright Co. builds to them.
11. No stock photos and no AI-generated images of land, lots, machines, crews or people presented as Wright Co. work. No photograph of a real Louisiana job exists yet, so every layout uses a labelled photo slot.
12. No green, no safety yellow, no blue or any cool color as a brand color. No pill-shaped buttons. No gradients.
13. The emblem is composited from the supplied files only. Never redrawn, re-lettered, recolored, traced or generated.

### The idea in brief

Someone buys a few wooded acres around Hammond to build a house, and finds that the builder's bid says "site work not included". The dirt side turns into three or four separate outfits: one to clear, one for stumps, one for the driveway and culvert, one for the pad. Each waits on the last and blames the others when something is off. Cody's own description of the work answers that: "We come in and clear your land, trees and stumps. Then we put in a driveway to the future home site. Then we excavate for the house foundation." One crew, in order, to a lot the builder can start on. The brand leads with that finish line ("build-ready") and that sequence, because those are things Wright Co. can show, not badges it would have to claim.

### Positioning statement (derived)

> For people around Hammond who've bought raw land to build a home, Wright Co. is the home-site prep crew that takes your lot from woods to build-ready (cleared, driveway in, house pad built) with one crew, because we do the dirt side and only the dirt side, in order, with no hand-offs to subs who blame each other.

Internal. Not a headline. The public form is the tagline.

### Purpose (derived)

> Wright Co. exists so that someone who just bought raw land to build a home doesn't have to become a general contractor to get the dirt side done.

## Mission (derived)

Take raw Louisiana land and make it build-ready, with one crew from the first tree to the finished house pad. Every job starts with a walk of the land and a plain-language plan, runs in the right order without hand-offs, and ends with a clean site handed to the builder.

## Vision (derived)

Be the crew people around Hammond name first when someone says "I bought land, now what?"

## Brand personality (derived)

Rugged. Straight-talking. Dependable. Louisiana-proud. Hardworking.

**Archetype: Everyman first, Hero second.** Everyman in the voice, Hero in the work. Wright Co. talks like a neighbor with the equipment and works like a pro. The plain talk wins trust; the heavy type, the numbered steps (01 · 02 · 03 · 04) and a clear finish line carry the competence. The Hero never boasts.

"Louisiana-proud" is expressed visually, through the emblem's state silhouette, cattails and marsh grass. It is never a verbal claim: no "Louisiana born and raised", no local roots implied.

For design, that means: dark soil grounds, heavy collegiate headlines, square edges, one thing in rust per screen, and grit kept in the emblem and (once they exist) the photographs. Type, UI and layout stay clean enough to read in one look from a truck cab.

## Audience

Everything in this section is derived from research. Wright Co. has no customer list, reviews or job history in Louisiana yet. Names and quotes in the source personas are illustrations and are never published.

### Primary audience: raw-land home builders

People who have just bought, or are closing on, wooded or overgrown acreage around Hammond to build a home. Often first-time landowners moving out from Baton Rouge, the Northshore or metro New Orleans. They don't know the order of operations (911 address, culvert permit, clearing, driveway, pad, builder). Their builder or lender needs a pad by a date, and the site work is on them. They decide by phone and by referral, mostly on a phone screen.

### Secondary audience: small custom and spec builders

Builders running a handful of rural homes a year who need one reliable pad sub: a pad built to the survey, on the date, without babysitting. Reached by direct outreach and a one-sheet until there is local proof. No builder page until three to five Louisiana lots are done.

### Secondary audience: rural landowners

Owners of family tracts who need a pond, drainage, a washed-out driveway fixed, or a homesite cleared for the next generation. They want respect for the land and their money, and no ruts in a wet field.

### How to speak to each

| Customer | Cares most about | Lead with | Avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Raw-land home builder | Not being in over their head; a pad the builder accepts; no surprise bills; someone who answers | Their moment ("Just closed on land?") and the finish line, then the sequence | Unexplained jargon; anything that assumes they know the process; pad heights; prices; per-acre framing |
| Small builder | Pad to the survey, on the date; one name on the schedule | "One crew. Woods to build-ready." | Homeowner hand-holding; in-house hauling or culvert install until `CONFIRM BEFORE PUBLISHING`; implying Wright Co. sets elevations |
| Rural landowner | Respect, a straight answer, a clean finish | Care for the land and plain dealing | Fast talk; "growth" language; pushing the full package |

### Customer priorities

- A lot their builder can start on.
- One call, one scope, one crew.
- The order explained before work starts.
- A straight answer on what drives the cost.
- Someone who picks up or calls back.

### Customer concerns

- "I don't even know who to call first."
- Hidden costs and quotes that can't be compared.
- A pad built to the wrong height. (The surveyor and the parish set it. Wright Co. says so.)
- Wet-weather delays.
- A contractor who disappears halfway.

### Who the brand is not for

- People who want the cheapest per-acre mulching with no house planned.
- Tree-care-only jobs: trimming, a single tree near a house.
- Big commercial, subdivision or municipal site work.
- Anyone who needs permits, surveys or elevations done for them.

Design and copy must not attract them: no per-acre price language, no arborist or tree-care imagery, no commercial-site language, nothing that says "we handle your permits".

## Brand promise (`CONFIRM BEFORE PUBLISHING`)

> Written scope. Firm price. We call you back.

Every part is a promise only the owner can keep, and "firm price" needs a change-order rule (bad soil, extra fill, weather) before it can run. It sits under a headline, never as one. **The safe line until he answers:** "Straight answers, in plain words. Call or text Cody."

| 4 | **Dirt side only** | No concrete, fencing, decking or tree care. "We stop where your builder starts." | Whether retaining walls, shop dig-outs, utility taps and trenching are offered is `CONFIRM BEFORE PUBLISHING`; none is shown until then |

"Clearing and excavating since 2022" supports trust. It is not a differentiator, and it never has a place attached.

## What the brand refuses to be

- A badge-and-superlatives brand: no "premier", no "#1", no trust-badge grid.
- A general contractor: no concrete, fencing, decking or tree care. We stop where the builder starts.
- A per-acre mulching shop.
- A brand that borrows proof: no Washington reviews or photos, no stock or AI job photos, no unverified credentials.
- A brand that names or knocks competitors.
- A brand that promises what the weather, the parish or the surveyor controls.

## Design principles

1. **Show the ground, not the gear.** Lead with the land and the result (a lot, a driveway, a pad), never a bare machine. With no real photo yet, the answer is type, the sequence and soil texture, never a borrowed picture.
2. **Heavy and square.** Graduate capitals, square buttons, chamfered cards, rust rules. Rounded, light or airy elements read as someone else's brand.
3. **One thing in rust.** Cattail Rust marks the action. If a screen has three rust things, two of them are wrong.
4. **Grit lives in the logo and the photos.** Distressing and illustration stay in the emblem and real job imagery. Running type and UI stay clean.
5. **Real or nothing.** A lot, a machine, a crew member or a customer on the page is a real Louisiana Wright Co. photograph. None exists yet, so the space is a labelled photo slot.
6. **One clear next step.** "Book a Free Lot Walk" as the solid primary button, "Call or Text 360-957-5865" as the outlined secondary, at equal width.

## Brand do

- Lead with the buyer's moment and the finish line: "You bought the land. We'll get it ready to build."
- Show the sequence the same way every time: 01 Clear · 02 Driveway · 03 Pad · 04 Foundation Dig.
- Composite the supplied emblem files, unchanged, on a dark ground or inside a square-cornered Soil container.
- Write "Wright Co." with the period.
- Name Cody as the person: "Call or text Cody."
- Use the place line: "Serving Hammond, Tangipahoa Parish & the Northshore". Name real towns in captions and local pages.
- Answer "how much?" with what drives the cost, then the Free Lot Walk.
- Say who sets the pad height: "Your surveyor and the parish set the height. We build to it."
- Use the honest-gap line while proof is thin: "We're new to the Hammond area, so we're showing our work one lot at a time."
- Use "Book a Free Lot Walk" and "Call or Text 360-957-5865", equal width, primary first.
- Keep Soil Black as the page and Bone for long reading sections. Rust for the action only.
- Put a labelled photo slot wherever a photograph belongs.
- Keep every unapproved item marked `CONFIRM BEFORE PUBLISHING`.

## Brand do not

- Redraw, re-letter, recolor, trace, stretch, tilt or add anything to the emblem. Do not set "WRIGHT CO" in Graduate or any typeface as a stand-in for the logo file. Do not imitate the emblem's distressing as a font effect.
- Put "LLC", a tagline, a phone number or "Est. 20XX" inside a logo lockup.
- Put the emblem straight onto Bone, white or a busy photograph.
- Use a stock photo, an AI-generated lot, machine, crew, landowner or scene, a cartoon, or another company's equipment.
- Use any photograph, review or testimonial from the owner's Washington business.
- Write "licensed", "insured", "bonded", "LLC", a license number, "established", "years in Hammond", "decades of experience" or "founded".
- Show a star rating, a review count, a review-seal row or a counter.
- Print a price, a range, a per-acre rate, a discount, "affordable" or "no hidden fees".
- Print a street address or put a pin on a map.
- Use "#1", "premier", "best", "trusted", "top-rated", "solutions", "comprehensive", "full-service", "no job too big or too small", "built on hard work" or "quality" as a claim.
- Use a competitor's phrasing: "standing pine", "slab", "every phase", "one contractor", or a bare "start to finish".
- Quote a pad height or elevation, promise a date or a dry pad, or say permits are "handled for you".
- Name or describe a competitor: no "unlike", "the other guys", "most companies".
- Add green, safety yellow, blue or any cool hue. Use a gradient. Use a pill-shaped or rounded button.
- Use Oswald, a condensed grotesk, a script, a western novelty face or a grunge font.
- Use Cajun or bayou costume: gators, crawfish, "cher", fake dialect.
- Make the phone a bare text link in a call-to-action block.
- Use more than one "Wright" pun anywhere in the system. "The Wright way" is held until the owner confirms it is his line: `CONFIRM BEFORE PUBLISHING`.
