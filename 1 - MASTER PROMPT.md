# 3. Brand foundation (agency proposal, `CONFIRM BEFORE PUBLISHING`)

## The visual idea
**The page should look like the ground itself, and like a local crew's truck door rather than a franchise website: dark soil, rust and bone, heavy collegiate lettering, square edges.** Soil Black carries the page. Bone carries the long reading. Cattail Rust marks the one thing to press. With eyes half shut, a page reads as dark earth with bone type and one rust button.

## Core message (the anchor, not a tagline)
"Wright Co. helps people around Hammond who've bought land to build a home get it build-ready, by sending one crew to clear it, cut in the driveway and build the house pad, in order, with no hand-offs." Every headline traces back to its four parts: the finish line (build-ready), the sequence (clear → driveway → pad), one crew (no hand-offs) and the place (the Hammond area).

## Positioning statement (internal, never a headline)
For people around Hammond who've bought raw land to build a home, Wright Co. is the home-site prep crew that takes your lot from woods to build-ready (cleared, driveway in, house pad built) with one crew, because we do the dirt side and only the dirt side, in order, with no hand-offs to subs who blame each other. **In four words: build-ready, one crew, in order.**

## Purpose
"Wright Co. exists so that someone who just bought raw land to build a home doesn't have to become a general contractor to get the dirt side done."

## Mission
Take raw Louisiana land and make it build-ready, with one crew from the first tree to the finished house pad. Every job starts with a walk of the land and a plain-language plan, runs in the right order without hand-offs, and ends with a clean site handed to the builder.

## Vision
Be the crew people around Hammond name first when someone says "I bought land, now what?"

## Values, in this order (the order follows the work)
1. **Start to finish, build-ready.** We own the whole dirt sequence, clearing through pad, so there's no gap between subs for a problem to fall into. ("Start to finish" never appears without "build-ready" or the sequence attached.)
2. **Straight answers.** You hear what drives the cost before you hear a number. When it's the surveyor's or the parish's call, we say so.
3. **Show up.** Returning a call is treated as part of the job. The callback standard itself is `CONFIRM BEFORE PUBLISHING`.
4. **Know the ground.** South Louisiana land is wet, clay-heavy and often near a flood zone. We learn each lot before we quote it.
5. **Leave it right.** The job's done when the site is clean and the builder can start, not when the machine stops.

## Personality
Rugged. Straight-talking. Dependable. Louisiana-proud. Hardworking. **Archetype: Everyman in the voice, Hero in the work.** It talks like a neighbor with the equipment and works like a pro. The heavy type, the numbered steps and a clear finish line carry the competence; the Hero never boasts. "Louisiana-proud" is carried by the emblem's state silhouette, cattails and marsh grass. It is never a verbal claim: no "Louisiana born and raised", no local roots implied.

## Brand promise (`CONFIRM BEFORE PUBLISHING`)
"Written scope. Firm price. We call you back." Every part is a promise only the owner can keep, and "firm price" needs a change-order rule first. It sits under a headline, never as one, and only with the badge. **The safe line, usable now:** "Straight answers, in plain words. Call or text Cody."

## Four pillars
1. **One crew does the whole dirt side.**
2. **Done in the right order, and we'll show you the order.** 01 Clear · 02 Driveway · 03 Pad · 04 Foundation Dig.
3. **Straight answers before a number.**
4. **Real lots, start to finish.** It has no proof yet. Until the first Louisiana job is photographed it runs only as the honest-gap line: "We're new to the Hammond area, so we're showing our work one lot at a time."

No pillar is shown without its proof. The proof for each is in file 5; pillar 4 renders as the honest-gap line and a labelled before/after slot pair, never as a gallery.

## Tagline and descriptors
- **Tagline:** "You bought the land. We'll get it ready to build." Decided by BlakSheep Creative and usable now; the owner's approval is pending. Sentence case with its period in source text; Graduate capitals in display. Never part of the logo artwork, never inside the emblem's clear space.
- **Short form** (hats, decals, tight spaces): "Ready to build."
- **Alternate for placements** (truck door, yard sign, under the emblem): "From the first tree to the house pad."
- **Builder-facing only:** "One crew. Woods to build-ready."
- **Held:** "Ready to build. The Wright way." `CONFIRM BEFORE PUBLISHING`: only if the owner confirms "the Wright way" is his line, and then one use in the whole system, as a sign-off or apparel line. Build it as an empty toggled slot, off by default.
- **Retired:** "Built on Hard Work. Driven by Quality." and "Work Done the Wright Way" as a heading. They go on nothing.

## Design principles
1. **Show the ground, not the gear.** Lead with the land and the result (a lot, a driveway, a pad), never a bare machine. With no real photo yet, the answer is type, the sequence and soil texture, never a borrowed picture.
2. **Heavy and square.** Graduate capitals, square buttons, chamfered cards, rust rules. Rounded, light or airy elements read as someone else's brand.
3. **One thing in rust.** Cattail Rust marks the action. If a screen has three rust things, two of them are wrong.
4. **Grit lives in the logo and the photos.** Distressing and illustration stay in the emblem and, once they exist, real job photographs. Running type and UI stay clean enough to read in one look from a truck cab.
5. **Real or nothing.** A lot, a machine, a crew member or a customer on the page is a real Louisiana Wright Co. photograph. None exists yet, so the space is a labelled photo slot.
6. **One clear next step.** "Book a Free Lot Walk" solid, "Call or Text 360-957-5865" outlined, at equal width.

## What the brand refuses to be
- A badge-and-superlatives brand: no "premier", no "#1", no trust-badge grid.
- A general contractor: no concrete, fencing, decking or tree care. "We stop where your builder starts."
- A per-acre mulching shop.
- A brand that borrows proof: no stock or AI job photos, no unverified credentials, nothing from the owner's other business.
- A brand that names, describes or knocks any competitor.
- A brand that promises what the weather, the parish or the surveyor controls.

- **It ships in one weight, 400, which already reads bold.** Load it as `family=Graduate` only; a request for `wght@700` fails. **Every Graduate element sets `font-weight: 400` and `font-synthesis: none`** so no browser or tool fakes a bold or an oblique. Never apply `<strong>`, `font-weight: 700` or italic to Graduate.
- **It is wide.** Uppercase only (via CSS `text-transform`, source text stays sentence case), six to eight words at most, never more than two lines.
- **Never** for H3, buttons, navigation, form labels, captions or paragraphs.
- It is a typeface, not the logo. "WRIGHT CO" is never set in it as a logo.

**Source Sans 3** is neutral and very legible on a phone in the sun, and supplies the weights Graduate lacks: 400, 600 and 700. No italics for emphasis. No weights below 400.

In Office, Canva or Google Docs without the brand fonts: headings in Rockwell Bold, falling back to Arial Black; body in Arial. Graduate is free, so send it with every print and vinyl job.

**Avoid:** Oswald or any condensed grotesk; distressed or grunge fonts in running text; script or western novelty faces; thin or light weights; all-caps paragraphs; a third typeface.
