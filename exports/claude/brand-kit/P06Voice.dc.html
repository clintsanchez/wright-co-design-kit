Section 02 · Brand law

Voice and messaging

Wright Co. sounds like the neighbor with the equipment who walks your land with you: plain-spoken, straight about the hard parts, and quietly sure he can get it ready to build.

Where it comes from. The owner’s own description of the work is the voice: “We come in and clear your land, trees and stumps. Then we put in a driveway to the future home site. Then we excavate for the house foundation.” Keep the “we come in”, the “then… then”, the “your land” and the zero hype. Fix the grammar only.

Rule 1

Walk the order. First, then, then. The product is the sequence, so the copy is a walkthrough.

Rule 2

Show it, don’t claim it. Specifics and, later, real lots carry the confidence. No adjectives about ourselves.

Rule 3

Describe only Wright Co. No sentence says what another outfit does or fails to do.

Tone dimensions
Casual to formalCasual, 2 of 8. Contractions always. “Folks” fine; “y’all” sparingly, social and spoken only
Calm to energeticSteady, 4 of 8. Energy lives in verbs: clear, cut, haul, dig, pad up
Serious to wittyMostly serious. Dry lines on Facebook only; never about money, weather or water
Accessible to authoritativeAccessible, 2 of 8. Use the trade word, then explain it once
Distant to intimateNeighborly, 5 of 8. “You” and “your land”. Name Cody, not “our team”

Mechanics. Sentences 8 to 15 words on the web, under 10 in social. “We” for the company, “you” for the reader, “I” only when Cody is literally writing. “Cody Wright” first, “Cody” after. Sentence case in source. No exclamation mark in a heading or button, one per page at most. No ellipses. Arrows and middots are house style. Explain trade words once: “a culvert (the pipe under your driveway so the ditch keeps draining)”. Numerals with a space: “3 acres”. American spelling.
